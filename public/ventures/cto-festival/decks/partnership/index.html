02 · The gap
The gap

Most technology events are not built for the people building it.

Two years of running the CTO Network taught us what the room is missing, and the festival is the answer to exactly these three.

Built for the wrong room.Conferences are programmed around chief executives, investors, and the people selling to them. Technical leaders end up in panels that perform.
Drowning in noise.Recruiter spam, vendor pitches, surface-level debate. The signal that matters, how peers are actually shipping, never reaches the people who need it.
No shared frontier.AI, regulation, infrastructure shifts, European technology sovereignty. The hardest questions of the next decade, and no forum built for them.

04 · The one rule
The one rule

The room is worth being in because of what we do not sell.

This is not a restriction we apologise for, it is the product. It is why two thousand four hundred technical leaders answer when the network writes to them, and it is why a partner brand is safe standing next to it. We say it out loud, and we hold to it on the day.

No list, ever.No attendee data leaves the building for cold outreach, and no mass mailing afterwards.
No pitch from the stage.Partners send a senior technical voice rather than a sales lead.
Introductions, opt-in.Connections happen in the room, and through matching people agree to.

08 · Twelve hours to takeoff
Twelve hours to takeoff · the night before

Fifty engineers. One airframe. Twelve hours.

The brief is to give it a mind of its own and get it airborne before the doors open. The whole night is filmed and cut before sunrise, and that film opens the festival.

20:00Clock starts. One airframe, fifty engineers, one brief.
23:40First dead end. The controller that was correct in simulation.
03:10The rewrite. Spec first this time. Agents write, humans argue.
06:55It lifts, or it does not. Latency is physical at this hour.
09:00Doors open. The cut is finished. The film plays.

Partners do not sponsor the night, they are in it. Their hardware, models and compute go into the build, and their engineers into the room as mentors.

09 · How the night works
How the night works

Optional, capped, and the part people talk about.

The build runs the evening before the festival and nobody has to be in it. The fifty who are get one real problem and a hard deadline at sunrise, and everybody else meets the night as the film that opens the day.

Real problems, real teams.One real challenge on the table, brought by a partner or sourced from the network. No toy briefs, real constraints, real data, real shipping decisions.
A curated room.Around fifty hands-on CTOs and senior engineers, capped on purpose. Mixed teams of four or five, paired across companies and stacks, so nobody builds with the people they message all week.
It opens the festival.The cut plays at kick-off in front of five hundred people, and the teams take the stage for questions. Builders judged by builders.
